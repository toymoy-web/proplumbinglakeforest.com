Why Is My IRWD Water Bill So High in Lake Forest, CA?

By the Lake Forest Pro Plumbing team. Published October 3, 2026. Updated October 3, 2026.

A high Irvine Ranch Water District bill usually comes from one of three causes: a leak, irrigation that runs past your budget, or a budget that does not fit your household. IRWD bills in budget tiers, so water used above your monthly budget costs several times more per unit. Check the meter first, then the toilets, then the sprinklers.

How IRWD's Budget-Based Billing Works

Irvine Ranch Water District serves most of Lake Forest, and it does not bill one price for every unit of water. Each household gets a monthly water budget, which is an indoor budget plus an outdoor budget. The price per unit climbs as you use more of it. Staying inside the budget keeps the bill low, and going past it is what makes a bill jump.

The outdoor budget is based on a drought-tolerant landscape watered by drip or high-efficiency irrigation, and it moves with the weather. It rises in hot or windy months and falls when it is cool or rainy. A lawn will usually push a household past its outdoor budget, so a high bill in July does not always mean a leak.

Water is measured in CCF, one hundred cubic feet or about 748 gallons. If El Toro Water District or Trabuco Canyon Water District serves your address instead, your rates and rules differ, so start with the name on your bill.

Rates change each year, so use IRWD's current rate sheet and bill calculator for today's numbers. The pattern is what matters: each step up costs several times more than the one before.

The Three Usual Causes of a High Bill

A leak is the first suspect, and the toilet is the most common source. According to IRWD, a silent toilet leak can waste about 40 gallons a day and a running toilet can waste up to five gallons a minute. A meter that keeps moving with every fixture off means water is leaving the system somewhere, either inside the house or on the buried line.

A leak detection visit finds where it is leaving, and a leak on the buried line from the meter to the house is covered under water service line repair.

Irrigation is second. A timer that resets after a power outage can fall back to wasteful default settings, and a broken sprinkler head can run unnoticed for weeks. Third is a budget that does not fit the household, such as more people living in the home than the district assumed.

A 20-Minute Check Before You Call Anyone

  1. Read the meter

    Turn off all water, then check whether the dial or low-flow indicator is still moving. If it is, water is escaping.

  2. Dye-test the toilets

    Put a few drops of food coloring in each tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a leak.

  3. Run irrigation by zone

    Watch each zone for puddles, geysers, and wet spots that stay wet after the water stops.

  4. Compare to last year

    Your online account lets you compare usage to the same month a year ago, which separates a seasonal change from a new problem.

How to Get a Leak Adjustment from IRWD

IRWD has a stated policy for leaks. After you find and repair a leak, if your usage returns to within budget, the district re-bills the usage that fell in the Inefficient and Wasteful tiers at the base rate. You still pay for the water, but at the lower rate.

Keep the repair invoice or a written finding from the plumber, then contact IRWD customer service at 949-453-5300 or CustomerService@IRWD.com and ask how to submit it. The adjustment does not pay for the repair itself. IRWD also offers a Water Insight program that can alert you to possible leaks before the next bill arrives.

When the Budget Itself Is Wrong

A budget can be too low for a real household. IRWD lets customers request a water variance to account for the number of occupants and landscaping, and its Water Efficiency team offers home audits. If you have no leak and no excess irrigation but still land in the upper tiers, a variance request is the next step.

In newer planned communities such as Baker Ranch, the association handles common-area irrigation, and your own yard drives your outdoor budget. A bill that rises and falls with the weather is usually irrigation. A bill that jumps and stays high is usually a leak.

What a Leak Does to the Bill: The Arithmetic

Numbers make the stakes concrete. A silent toilet leak of 40 gallons a day adds up to 1,200 gallons in a month, which is about 1.6 CCF. Even if all of it landed in the Wasteful tier at the April 2025 rate, that is roughly $26 a month. It is real money over a year, but it rarely causes a shock.

A running toilet is a different matter. At five gallons a minute it moves 7,200 gallons a day, or about 9.6 CCF a day. If a household is already over budget, each of those units is billed in the upper tiers. That is how a few days of an unnoticed fault turns into a bill of hundreds of dollars.

These figures are illustrations using the tier rates quoted above, and your own bill depends on your budget and on how much of the extra use falls above it. The point is the shape: a small leak is a slow drain on your wallet, and a big one is a fast one.

Reading the Rest of the Bill

The tiers are only the variable part. The IRWD bill also carries fixed charges for water service and for sewer service, and the water service charge is based on the size of your meter. Those charges do not change when you use more or less water, so they explain why a bill never falls to zero.

When you compare this month with last year, compare usage first, not dollars. A rate change can move the dollar amount even when the water use is flat. If usage rose and the weather explains it, no leak is likely. If usage rose in a cool month with no change at home, look harder.

Lake Forest Note: IRWD Serves Most of the City, Not All of It

IRWD's own water quality report says it serves all of Irvine and most of Lake Forest, while El Toro Water District and Trabuco Canyon Water District serve other parts. The name on your bill tells you which rules apply, and homes in Portola Hills and Trabuco Canyon may not be IRWD customers at all.

IRWD says that when a customer repairs a leak and usage returns to budget, it rebills the over-budget usage from the Inefficient and Wasteful tiers at the base rate. The customer still pays for the water, so a leak adjustment lowers the bill without erasing it, and a written finding from leak detection helps when you ask.

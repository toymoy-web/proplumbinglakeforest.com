Soggy Yard, Low Pressure, or a Meter That Spins With the House Off: Is It the Service Line?

The service line is the pipe that brings water from the district's meter to your house. It is buried, so a leak shows up indirectly. A soggy patch in the lawn or driveway, pressure that dropped, or a meter that keeps turning with everything off are the usual signs.

A simple test separates the service line from the pipes inside the house. Close the main shutoff at the house and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside, and the first call is a leak detection visit.

If the line is the problem, the repair is on your side of the meter. The district's meter, shutoff, and the main in the street belong to the water district.

Who Is Responsible for What

Responsibility generally splits at the meter. The district owns and maintains the main and the meter, and the owner is responsible for the pipe from the meter to the house. Lake Forest is served by several districts, including Irvine Ranch Water District and El Toro Water District, so check your bill for which one is yours.

If the leak is on the district's side, call the district. If the leak is on yours, it is a plumbing job. The district can also tell you whether it will adjust your bill after a documented repair. IRWD, for example, re-bills over-budget use at a lower rate after a leak is repaired.

Digging in the street or the public right-of-way usually needs a permit, and the city or district will tell you who issues it. Digging on your own property also often needs a permit, and a plumber should say who pulls it.

Where Lake Forest Water Lines Fail

Age is the main cause. The older El Toro neighborhoods date to the 1970s and 1980s, so their service lines are four to five decades old. Older lines can be galvanized steel or copper, and the plumber can tell you which you have when the line is exposed.

Hillside lots in Portola Hills can see higher pressure and ground movement, both of which stress buried pipe. Tree roots, landscaping work, and nearby construction can also damage a line.

Pressure matters. If the pressure at your house exceeds 80 psi, a regulator is needed under the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A failing regulator can also overstress a service line.

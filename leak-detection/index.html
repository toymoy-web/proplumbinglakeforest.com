Water Bill Up and Nothing Dripping? How Hidden Leaks Show Up

Most hidden leaks announce themselves through the water bill or the meter long before they stain a ceiling. These are the signs worth acting on.

The meter moves with everything off

If the dial or low-flow indicator keeps turning when no one is using water, the water is going somewhere.

The bill jumped and habits did not

A rise with no change in showers, laundry, or irrigation points to a leak or a stuck fixture.

A warm spot on the floor

Hot-water lines under a slab can warm the flooring above them.

Water sounds at night

Hissing or trickling in a quiet house often means a pressurized line is leaking.

Damp baseboards or a musty smell

Moisture wicks upward and shows at the base of walls first.

Pressure that sagged

A drop in pressure with nothing else changed can mean a break between the meter and the house.

The Two-Minute Meter Test and What Your District Offers

Start with the meter. Irvine Ranch Water District's own guidance is to turn off all water and check whether the dial is still spinning. If it is, you probably have a leak. Check the obvious source first, because IRWD notes that a leaking toilet can waste about 40 gallons a day.

A meter that moves with everything off tells you a leak exists. It does not tell you where. The next question is whether the water escapes before the house shutoff, on the service line, or inside the house. Closing the main shutoff and watching the meter again separates the two.

Your district may also help with the bill. IRWD bills on a water budget, and its stated policy is that after a leak is repaired and usage returns to budget, the over-budget usage is re-billed at the base rate. Keep the repair invoice and the findings report. El Toro Water District and Trabuco Canyon Water District set their own policies, so ask yours which applies.

Outside the House: Service Lines and Sprinklers

The water service line runs from the district's meter to your house, and the section after the meter is generally the owner's responsibility. A leak there can run for months under a lawn or driveway with no wet spot on the surface, because the soil absorbs it. Low pressure and a moving meter are the usual clues.

Irrigation is the other outdoor suspect. IRWD's guidance for a high bill lists a sprinkler test and a timer check, since a power outage can reset a timer to wasteful defaults. A technician can tell a broken sprinkler head from a supply-line leak by isolating the irrigation line and watching the meter again. A leaking pool is a separate case, covered under pool leak detection, and irrigation assemblies need yearly checks under backflow testing.

Slab-on-grade homes, including the newer Baker Ranch tracts, run some lines in or under the concrete. When a slab leak is suspected, the goal is to pinpoint it to a few square feet so the repair opens a small area instead of a whole room. If the wet spot turns out to be at the tank, see water heater replacement.

What Happens During a Leak Detection Visit

Any repair that follows is done to the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

  1. Confirm

    A meter test and a few questions about symptoms show whether a leak exists.

  2. Isolate

    Shutoffs split the house, the yard, and the irrigation so each can be tested alone.

  3. Pinpoint

    Listening and thermal tools narrow the leak to a small area.

  4. Report

    You get the location, the method used, a recommended repair, and a price.

What to Do Before the Technician Arrives

A few minutes of preparation makes the visit faster and gives your insurer and your water district what they ask for.

  • 1
    Photograph the meterRecord the reading and the time, then again an hour later with all water off.
  • 2
    Run the toilet dye testA few drops of food coloring in each tank, left for fifteen minutes, show whether color leaks into the bowl.
  • 3
    Note the sounds and the spotsWrite down where you hear water and where floors or walls feel warm or damp.
  • 4
    Leave walls and slab aloneOpening a wall or floor before the leak is pinpointed turns a small repair into a large one.
  • 5
    Keep every invoiceThe findings report and the repair receipt support both an insurance claim and a district bill adjustment.

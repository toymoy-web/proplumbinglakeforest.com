How Hard Is Lake Forest Water?

Water in Lake Forest comes from imported supplies, treated by the districts that serve the city. Published hardness figures for the area run from moderately hard to hard, depending on the source and the district. The district's annual water quality report is the best place to look for your own figure.

Hardness is measured in grains per gallon, and softener size depends on it. A plumber can also test the water at your tap, since the number can differ from the district average. A unit sized for the wrong hardness wastes salt or fails to soften enough.

Hard water also shortens the life of fixtures and appliances. See tank water heater repair for what scale does to a tank, and tankless water heater service for what it does to a tankless unit.

How a Water Softener Installation Goes

The plumbing connections follow the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

  1. Test and size

    Hardness, household size, and water use set the size of the unit.

  2. Choose the location

    Most are installed in a garage or utility area near the main line.

  3. Install

    The unit is plumbed in with a bypass valve, a drain connection, and power.

  4. Program and verify

    The unit is set for your hardness and the softened water is tested.

Salt, Salt-Free, and Local Rules

A traditional softener uses ion exchange. Resin beads trap hardness minerals and are recharged with salt, and the wastewater carries the salt to the sewer. Some California communities limit or ban salt-discharging softeners because of what the salt does to recycled water, so ask your district or city what applies to your address.

Look for a softener certified to NSF/ANSI Standard 44, which covers residential cation exchange softeners and tests the capacity and efficiency a maker claims. A salt-free conditioner works differently. It does not remove the minerals but changes how they form scale. Results vary, and it is not a true softener, so the choice depends on what you want to fix.

Softened water adds sodium, in amounts that depend on the hardness. If anyone in the home is on a sodium-restricted diet, ask a physician. We do not make health claims, and a separate drinking-water filter can address taste.

What to Do If Your Softener Stopped Working

Before calling, a few checks often find the cause.

  • 1
    Salt levelAn empty brine tank is the most common cause. Fill it and wait for the next regeneration.
  • 2
    Salt bridge or mushA hard crust or sludge in the brine tank blocks salt from dissolving. Breaking it up helps.
  • 3
    Power and settingsA power outage can reset the clock and regeneration schedule.
  • 4
    The bypass valveIf it was left in bypass, the unit is not softening.
  • 5
    Resin ageResin beads wear out after many years. If the unit is old and hardness returns, the resin may need replacing.

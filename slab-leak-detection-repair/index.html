Warm Floor Spot or a Meter That Will Not Stop: Slab Leak Signs

A slab leak hides under flooring, so the signs show up indirectly. Any one of these is worth a call. Two or more together make a slab leak likely.

A warm spot on the floor

A leaking hot-water line can warm the slab and the flooring above it.

A water heater that runs constantly

A hot line leaking under the slab makes the heater work to refill and reheat.

The sound of running water

Hissing or trickling with all fixtures off points to a pressurized leak.

Wet or buckled flooring

Water coming up through the slab lifts laminate and wood and dampens carpet.

Moisture at the base of walls

Water wicks into baseboards and drywall from below.

A meter that moves with everything off

This is the simplest check and often the first confirmation.

Slab Leak Emergency: When to Call Right Now

Some slab leaks are slow and some are not. Call the 24/7 line right away if water is coming up through the floor, the floor feels warm or looks wet, or you hear water running with every fixture off.

What a Slab Leak Is and Why It Matters

A slab leak is a break in a pressurized water line that runs beneath or inside the concrete foundation slab. Because the line runs under the slab, water can run for weeks before the floor shows it. Meanwhile it can erode the soil under the slab, raise the water bill, and soak the structure from below.

Slab-on-grade construction is common in southern California, and it is the standard for newer communities such as Baker Ranch. Older tracts in the El Toro core and the 1990s communities of Foothill Ranch also include slab homes. Where the plumbing runs depends on the builder and the era, so a plumber confirms by testing, not by assumption.

General leak finding explains how a hidden leak is found throughout a house. This page focuses on the slab, because the repair choices there are different and costlier.

Four Ways to Fix a Slab Leak

There is no single right repair. The best choice depends on how many leaks the line has had, how old the pipe is, and what sits above the slab.

Emergency Steps While You Wait for the Slab Leak Crew

A slab leak is rarely a flood, but it is wasteful and it keeps working. These steps limit the cost while you schedule a plumber.

  • 1
    Close the main shutoffWith the water off, the leak stops. Reopen only for short periods when you need water.
  • 2
    Turn off the water heaterA leaking hot line can keep refilling the tank. Switch off the heater so it does not run on an empty or draining tank.
  • 3
    Record the meterPhotograph the reading, then again an hour later with all water off.
  • 4
    Move belongings off the floorIf flooring is damp, lift rugs and furniture to prevent staining and mold.
  • 5
    Do not break the slab yourselfCutting concrete over a pressurized line is dangerous and can damage nearby pipes and electrical conduit.

Insurance and Slab Leaks: What Is Usually Covered

Coverage varies, and the fine print matters here. Many policies cover the cost of accessing a pipe after a sudden and accidental leak, which can include breaking the slab, but not the cost of the pipe itself. Policies often exclude leaks that developed gradually over time.

A written detection report helps. It records when you found the leak, where it is, and how you confirmed it. Send it to your agent before work begins, and ask whether tear-out and floor replacement are covered.

If water has already reached the flooring or drywall, a mitigation crew can start the drying while the pipe repair is planned.

From Detection to Repair

  1. Confirm

    A meter test and a pressure test show whether a leak exists under the slab.

  2. Isolate

    Shutting off sections of the house narrows which line is involved.

  3. Pinpoint

    Listening and thermal tools locate the leak to a small area from the surface.

  4. Choose

    You see the spot repair, reroute, and repipe options, each with a price.

  5. Repair and test

    The crew does the work, pressure tests the line, and checks the meter.

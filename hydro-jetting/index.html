Snaked Twice and It Still Clogs? When Jetting Is the Answer

A drain that clogs again a few weeks after it was cleared is usually not a new clog. A cable machine drills a hole through the blockage and leaves the rest of the buildup stuck to the pipe walls. The line stays narrow, and the next bit of grease or paper catches on what remains.

Roots behave the same way. A cable cuts the root where it enters the pipe, but the root grows back from the stump. Hydro jetting pushes water through the pipe at high pressure and scours the walls, which clears far more of the buildup than a cable can.

That does not make jetting right for every line. The next sections cover how it works, when to skip it, and why a camera look comes first.

What High-Pressure Water Does Inside a Pipe

A jetting machine pumps water through a flexible hose to a nozzle. Jets angled backward pull the hose forward through the line, while the spray scours the pipe walls and flushes debris toward the cleanout. Residential machines commonly work in the low thousands of psi.

Pressure alone is not the skill. The operator matches nozzle type, pressure, and flow rate to the pipe diameter and material, and uses a different nozzle for roots than for grease. Wastewater has to go somewhere, so a working cleanout and a clear path out of the line matter.

For a kitchen line on a restaurant or food court, jetting is often a scheduled task and not an emergency. The same approach applies to the shared grease lines covered under commercial plumbing service.

When Jetting Is the Wrong Tool

High-pressure water can finish off a pipe that is already failing. These are the cases that call for a camera look first and sometimes a different fix.

  • 1
    Cracked or collapsed pipeJetting a broken line can widen the damage and push debris into the break. Repair comes first, as covered under lateral repair and lining.
  • 2
    Fragile older pipeSome homes built before the 1970s have fiber conduit sewer pipe that softens with age. It does not tolerate high pressure.
  • 3
    Offset or separated jointsWater and debris escape at the gap, and the offset keeps catching new buildup.
  • 4
    Low spots that hold waterA sag in the line collects waste. Jetting clears it temporarily, but the sag stays.
  • 5
    A solid object in the lineA toy, a pipe fragment, or a failed fitting is better retrieved than blasted downstream.

How a Jetting Visit Runs

  1. Camera first

    A camera confirms the pipe is sound and shows what is coating it.

  2. Plan

    Nozzle, pressure, and access point are chosen for the pipe size and the buildup.

  3. Jet

    The line is cleaned in passes, working from the cleanout toward the main.

  4. Check

    A second camera pass confirms the walls are clear and notes any defects.

Built From 2014 Onward: Young Pipes, Real Questions

Developers recorded tract maps for the first seven Baker Ranch communities in 2013, and the first phase opened in 2014. The land had once been under the flight path of the former El Toro Marine base. Builders finished most homes in the late 2010s, so they are roughly 6 to 12 years old.

At that age, pipes rarely fail from wear. The calls tend to be faucets and toilets that wear early, a water heater with a defect, and workmanship problems that took years to show. Those are different from the age-related failures of older neighborhoods, and the answer to who pays can be different too.

Homes here sit on concrete slabs, so technicians find a water leak under the floor with electronic equipment and not by opening walls. That is covered under finding and fixing a slab leak.

Before You Pay for a Repair: Warranty and Defect Rights

A home this new may still carry rights worth checking before you pay a plumber. Builders give warranties, and California's construction defect law, the Right to Repair Act, sets standards for new homes and a process for claims. The details depend on your purchase date and the problem.

Photograph the problem and keep any failed part. Contact the builder's warranty line, and ask an attorney if the answer is unclear. If water is actively damaging the home, stopping it comes first, and a plumber's written finding of the cause helps any later claim.

Emergency repairs to stop damage are reasonable in any case. Just document them.

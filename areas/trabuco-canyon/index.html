Municipal or Private? Water and Sewer Systems in Trabuco Canyon

Trabuco Canyon is an unincorporated community in the foothills of the Santa Ana Mountains, partly within the Cleveland National Forest. Its plumbing situations vary more than anywhere else we dispatch. Trabuco Canyon Water District serves Trabuco Canyon, Robinson Ranch, Trabuco Highlands, Walden, Rancho Cielo, Portola Hills, Santiago Canyon Estates, and Dove Canyon.

Area residents voted to form the district in October 1962. Before that, the canyon was largely summer homes that drew water from wells, earthen reservoirs, creeks, and springs. In 1984 the district built a wastewater treatment plant together with the Robinson Ranch development. Some properties in the canyon still rely on private wells or septic systems, so the first question on a call is which system your home uses.

Your water bill and your property documents answer that. If you are unsure, tell the dispatcher what you know, and the first visit can confirm it.

Rural Roads and Long Service Lines

Many canyon parcels sit well back from the road, so the water line from the meter to the house can be long. A long buried line has more places to leak, and a leak can run for months under dirt and driveway without showing on the surface. A meter that moves with the house off, or pressure that has dropped, are the usual clues.

The canyon also has slopes and changes in elevation, which affect pressure from one property to the next. If pressure runs above 80 psi, the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a regulator. See the leak detection guide for how a hidden leak is found, and fixing the service line for the repair.

Active Leak in Trabuco Canyon: What to Do First

Canyon properties often have long supply runs and sometimes a private well, so the order matters.

Close the main valve

Find the house valve first. On long runs, the leak may be far from the house, so the meter test also matters.

Switch off a well pump

If you have a private well, turn off the pump breaker once the house valve is closed so the pump does not keep running.

Stop using water if sewage surfaces

Wastewater in the yard or backing up indoors means no flushing and no laundry until it is checked.

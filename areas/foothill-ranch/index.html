What Goes Wrong First in a Community Built Between 1990 and 1999

Foothill Ranch is young by Orange County standards and old enough to show its age. Hon Development bought the roughly 3,000-acre Whiting Ranch in 1987, physical development began in 1989, and the first homes went on sale in 1990. Crews finished nearly all of the initial construction by 1999, and the community joined the city of Lake Forest in 2000.

That timeline puts most homes at 25 to 35 years old. The original water heaters are long gone, and many houses are on a second or third unit. Supply lines, shutoff valves, and fixtures are at the age where leaks and seized valves begin to show, which is why many calls from 92610 start with a small drip or a valve that will not turn.

Higher Streets, Higher Pressure

Water pressure depends on elevation, and Foothill Ranch climbs into the hills above Whiting Ranch Wilderness Park. A house on a lower street can see much higher pressure than one near the ridge, because the water in the main is pushing against less height.

The California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), caps household pressure at 80 psi unless a regulator is installed. A cheap gauge on a hose bib reads it in seconds. A reading above 80 means the regulator is worn or missing, and high pressure is a common cause of the failures covered under emergency burst pipe repair.

Replacing a regulator is a small job compared with a flooded kitchen. Ask for the check whenever a plumber is in the house for another reason.

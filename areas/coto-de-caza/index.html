A Gated Community Built Over Four Decades

Coto de Caza is an unincorporated census-designated place, and the 2020 Census counted 14,710 residents. The project began in 1964, opened in 1986 under a master plan for about 5,000 homes, and finished building in 2003. Roughly 4,000 homes fall under a master association with its own security force.

A development that grew over nearly two decades has homes of several ages in one community. Expect some original water heaters and fixtures from the first phases, and newer systems in the last-built streets.

Los Ranchos Estates is a separate gated subcommunity of 75 custom homes on 355 acres. Custom homes often have longer pipe runs and larger systems, so ask the dispatcher about the size of the house.

Santa Margarita Water District Handles Water and Sewer

Santa Margarita Water District provides water and sewer service to Coto de Caza. The district serves more than 200,000 residents across about 100 square miles and was founded in 1964. Its Chiquita Water Reclamation Plant treats sewage for Rancho Santa Margarita, Coto de Caza, and part of Trabuco Canyon.

Responsibility generally splits at the meter, so a leak on the street side is the district's. A leak on the house side is yours, and a buried line there is covered under water line repair.

Pressure, pipe material, and well or septic status differ by street, so ask the plumber to check yours. Work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

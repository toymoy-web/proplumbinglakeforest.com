Hillside Lots, Slopes, and Water Pressure

Portola Hills backs up to Whiting Ranch Wilderness Park, and most lots sit on a slope. Slope affects plumbing in two ways. Water pressure changes with elevation, and runoff finds its way toward the lowest part of the property, which is often a garage or a slab edge.

Under the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), a pressure regulator is required wherever static pressure would exceed 80 psi. If your house sits well below the street, check it. A reading above 80 is hard on every fixture and supply line.

A meter that keeps moving with everything off is the first sign of a hidden leak. The leak detection page explains the test, and a burst line is covered under emergency pipe repair.

Two Generations of Homes in One ZIP

In an older home, expect aging water heaters, worn fixtures, and original shutoff valves. In a newer home, the pipes are young, and the first question after a leak is whether the builder's warranty applies. California's construction defect law, the Right to Repair Act, can apply to newer homes, so ask before you pay for a repair that may be a defect.

In either case, photograph the problem and keep any failed part. A written finding of the cause helps with a warranty claim.

That matters for two reasons. Responsibility generally splits at the meter, so a leak on the street side is the district's and a leak on the house side is yours. What to Do When a Storm Reaches Your Plumbing

Rain and plumbing leaks can look alike. These steps help you tell them apart and limit damage.

  • 1
    Check the timing of the stainA stain that appears only during or right after rain usually comes from the roof or drainage. A stain that grows in dry weather points to plumbing.
  • 2
    Look at the garage floor and slab edgesWater along a slab edge after rain is a drainage issue. Water that appears with the meter moving is a pipe.
  • 3
    Close the main shutoff if water is flowingIf a pipe is leaking, stop it first and call. If it is rain, keep the water on and fix the entry point.
  • 4
    Photograph before cleanupRecord the stain, the water, and the date. Insurers ask for it.
  • 5
    Call about drying if water soaked materialsWet drywall and flooring need drying within a day or two. See water mitigation.

Safety First: Gas, Carbon Monoxide, and Scalding

Most water heater problems are inconvenient. A few are dangerous, and these are the ones that should stop you from trying a fix.

  • 1
    A gas smellLeave the house, then call 911 and your gas utility from outside. Do not use switches or a phone inside.
  • 2
    Soot or scorch marks on the heaterThese can mean exhaust is backing into the room instead of leaving through the vent, which is a carbon monoxide risk.
  • 3
    A carbon monoxide alarmTake it seriously. Get everyone outside and call 911.
  • 4
    A leaking relief valveThe valve is a safety device. Never cap it or plug the discharge pipe, because a stuck tank can rupture.
  • 5
    Very hot water at the tapWater above about 120 degrees Fahrenheit can scald quickly, especially children. The thermostat should be set accordingly.

What Wears Out Inside a Tank Heater

A tank heater is simple, which is why a short list of parts accounts for most repairs. Electric units have two heating elements and two thermostats. Gas units have a burner, a pilot or igniter, a thermocouple or thermopile, and a gas control valve.

Every tank also has an anode rod, a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Hard water uses it up faster. Lake Forest water is treated by local districts, and published hardness figures run from moderately hard to hard, so checking the anode before it wears away can add years to the tank.

The relief valve, the drain valve, and the fittings at the top of the tank also wear. A leak from any of these is often repairable. A leak from the tank body is not, and that is the signal to look at water heater replacement.

How a Repair Visit Runs

Repairs and replaced parts follow the California Plumbing Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

  1. Listen

    The plumber asks about the symptom, the age of the unit, and what has already been tried.

  2. Test

    The plumber checks power, gas, thermostats, and elements or the burner before replacing any part.

  3. Quote

    You see the repair price and whether replacement would make more sense.

  4. Repair and verify

    The plumber replaces the part, brings the heater up to temperature, and checks the safety devices.

When Repair Stops Making Sense

A repair is worth it when the tank is sound and the cost is small compared with a new unit. A common rule is that a repair approaching half the cost of replacement, on a tank past eight years old, is better spent on a new heater.

Age matters because the part that fails next is usually the tank. If the repair price is close to replacement and the unit is near the end of its normal life, ask for both numbers in writing. A good plumber will give them without pressure.

Gas and electric tanks are not the only option. If the unit is old and the household has outgrown it, the plumber can explain tankless service and replacement as well, covered under tankless unit service.

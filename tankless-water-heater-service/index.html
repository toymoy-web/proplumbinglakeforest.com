Lukewarm Water, an Error Code, or a Unit That Needs Service: Is It Scale?

A tankless heater heats water as it flows through a heat exchanger with narrow passages. Minerals in hard water cling to those passages as scale and gradually choke them. The usual results are lukewarm water, a unit that shuts off mid-shower, and an error code that appears after a year or two.

Owners often say the unit just needs a service, or that it has not been cleaned since it was installed. They are usually right. Many homeowners also report that another company would only offer to replace the unit and would not service it. Descaling is a service most plumbers can do, and replacement is not the only answer.

Scale is not the only cause, so the table below covers the common alternatives.

What a Tankless Service Visit Includes

  1. Check the history

    The unit's error log and run hours show what has been going wrong.

  2. Flush the heat exchanger

    The technician closes the isolation valves and circulates a descaling solution through the unit.

  3. Clean the filters

    The technician cleans or replaces the inlet water filter and any gas or air screens.

  4. Test and verify

    The technician checks gas pressure, venting, flame, and temperature, and tests the condensate drain.

Hard Water and Tankless Units in Lake Forest

Published hardness figures for Lake Forest range from moderately hard to hard, depending on the source and the district. That makes scale a real maintenance item here. A common recommendation is to descale a tankless unit about once a year, and more often in harder water, so ask your plumber what your readings suggest.

The service depends on isolation valves, which are the shutoffs that let a plumber connect a pump to the unit. Some installations skip them. If yours has none, adding them is a small job that makes every later service possible.

A water softener or conditioner reduces scale throughout the house and helps the unit. It does not replace the flush, but it can stretch the time between services.

What to Check Before You Call

A few quick checks take a minute and sometimes save a visit. Stop and call if you smell gas or see water on the floor.

  • 1
    Power and gasMake sure the unit has power and that the gas valve is open.
  • 2
    The displayPhotograph any error code or read it to the dispatcher.
  • 3
    The inlet filterIf your model has an accessible filter, a clogged one can limit flow. Your manual shows the steps.
  • 4
    The flow at the tapA very low flow may not start the unit. Try a fixture with higher flow.
  • 5
    Water on the floorIf you see a leak, shut off the water and gas to the unit and call right away.

Service, Repair, or Replace?

Most tankless units last longer than tank models when they are maintained, but scale shortens that. A unit that has never been descaled can often be rescued by a flush, unless the heat exchanger is already damaged.

If service does not solve the problem, the plumber will tell you whether a part can be replaced or whether the unit has reached the end of its life. Any new installation follows the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Tank units are covered under water heater repair, and the replacement process is described under water heater installation and replacement.

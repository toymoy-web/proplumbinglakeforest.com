Emergency Under the Sink: Burst Supply Line, Broken Valve, or Spray

Water spraying from under a sink or a faucet that will not shut off is an emergency. Close the two small valves under the sink, turning them clockwise, and if they will not turn or one has snapped, close the main shutoff for the house.

A braided supply line that bursts, a shutoff valve that cracks when touched, and a handle that comes apart under pressure are the usual causes. Put a towel and a bucket under the leak, and tell the dispatcher what kind of fixture it is so the right parts are on the truck.

The same steps apply to a laundry sink, a utility sink, the valve behind a washing machine, and an outdoor hose spigot. Each has its own shutoff, and when none can be found, the main shutoff is the fallback.

Hard Water Wears Out Faucets Faster

Water in Lake Forest is treated by the local districts, and published hardness figures run from moderately hard to hard depending on the source. Minerals build up inside cartridges and clog aerators, which is why a faucet that worked fine for years can start to sputter or drip.

Cleaning an aerator with a vinegar soak is a safe first step. If the drip comes from the cartridge, replacement is cheap compared with a new faucet. If scale is a problem throughout the house, a whole-house softener addresses the cause instead of the symptom.

Older faucets in the 1970s and 1980s homes of the El Toro core may no longer have parts available. In that case, replacement costs about the same as a repair and gives you a newer, more efficient fixture.

Emergency: What to Do When a Sink Is Leaking

A leak under a sink is rarely an emergency, but it does get worse. These steps limit the damage.

  • 1
    Close the shutoff valvesLook for two small valves under the sink, one for hot and one for cold. Turn them clockwise. If they will not turn, close the main shutoff.
  • 2
    Empty the cabinetRemove stored items and dry the floor. Wet cardboard and cleaning supplies hold moisture.
  • 3
    Place a bucket under the dripA leaking trap or supply line can fill a bucket in an hour or two.
  • 4
    Look for the source before callingDry the pipes with a towel, then watch for the first bead of water. Telling the plumber where it starts saves time.
  • 5
    Photograph the damageIf the cabinet floor or wall is wet, take photos before cleanup in case you need to make a claim.

Choosing a Replacement Faucet

California sets flow limits on new faucets. Under the state's green building code, residential bathroom faucets are limited to 1.2 gallons per minute and kitchen faucets to 1.8 gallons per minute. A new faucet will not waste water the way a fifteen-year-old one might.

Check the hole count in your sink or countertop before buying. A single-hole faucet fits one opening, while a widespread set needs three. A faucet that does not match the sink needs a deck plate or a different sink.

If the drain is also slow, the clog may be below the trap. See drain cleaning and unclogging. A kitchen sink with a disposal has its own page under garbage disposal repair and installation. For a shower or tub valve, see shower and bathtub installation.

Tub and Shower Valves, Hose Spigots, and Laundry Valves

Faucet calls often include fixtures outside the kitchen and bath. A tub spout that leaks, a shower valve that drips behind the wall, and an outdoor hose spigot that sprays from the stem are common, and so is a leaking valve behind a washing machine.

Each of these has its own shutoff, and the right fix depends on the part. A cartridge replacement is usually a short visit, while a valve inside a wall may need access. Tell the dispatcher which fixture it is and whether the water stops when you close the shutoff.

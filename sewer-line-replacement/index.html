Repair or Replace? The Decision in Plain Terms

You can repair or line most laterals. Replacement is the answer when the pipe itself has stopped being a pipe. The camera report decides, and these are the findings that usually tip it.

What the camera showsWhat it meansUsual verdict
A collapsed or crushed sectionThe pipe has lost its shape and flowReplace the affected run
A deep sag that holds waterThe pipe has settled, so waste pools and cannot drainReplace, since lining follows the sag
Many cracks or offsets along the lineThe pipe is failing in many places at onceReplace, since spot repairs would never end
Fiber conduit pipe that has softenedMaterial from before the 1970s that deforms with ageReplace with modern pipe
Roots returning to many jointsThe joints no longer sealReplace, or line if the pipe is round and sound
One short damaged sectionLocal damage in an otherwise sound pipeRepair, not replacement

A single defect is a repair. Replacement is for pipe that is failing along its length.

What Replacement Actually Involves

The lateral runs from the cleanout at the house to the connection at the sewer main. Replacement swaps that run for new pipe that the crew installs to the California Plumbing Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The connection at the main belongs to the district, so that tie-in is coordinated with it, and the district or city sets the permit and inspection steps.

Two methods do the work. In a trench replacement, the crew digs out the old pipe and lays new pipe in its place. In a trenchless method, the crew pulls a new pipe through the old one while breaking the old pipe outward, which limits digging to pits at each end. Which one fits depends on the soil, the depth, and what sits above the line.

The shorter version of this job is covered under sewer line repair, which explains spot repair and lining. Replacement is the larger step, and the provider quotes it separately.

Replacement Methods Compared

From Camera to Final Inspection

  1. Inspect

    A camera run measures the damage and confirms whether the line needs replacement.

  2. Plan and quote

    The provider prices the method, the permit plan, and the surface restoration before work starts.

  3. Mark and permit

    The crew marks underground utilities through 811 and pulls permits.

  4. Replace and inspect

    The new lateral is installed and inspected, and a final camera run confirms it.

What to Do to Prepare for a Sewer Replacement

Replacement turns part of the property into a work zone for a few days. A little preparation avoids most of the surprises.

  • 1
    Plan around no water useWhile the line is open, drains cannot be used. Ask for a realistic window and plan showers and laundry around it.
  • 2
    Tell the crew what is above the lineIrrigation lines, tree roots, cables, and decorative features near the route should be pointed out beforehand.
  • 3
    Clear vehicles and gatesTrucks and digging equipment need access to the dig area.
  • 4
    Ask about restoration in writingConcrete, pavers, sod, and sprinklers may be included or not. Know before work starts.
  • 5
    Confirm who pulls the permitThe quote should say who obtains it and who pays the fee.
